## 1 · Proposal

The following proposal has been submitted to AktivInvest for consideration and funding:

**PM-AIgent** is an AI-based, multi-skill project management system built around an **agentic architecture** that can plan, staff, execute, monitor and hand over complex projects or entire programmes with minimal human oversight. Rather than sitting alongside classical PM tools as another dashboard, PM-AIgent replaces the project office as the coordinating layer — humans remain in decision and approval roles, the agent runs the loop.

---

## 2 · Product / Features

- **Optimize** — PM-AIgent draws on a domain-specific database of the customer's past and current projects to extract *lessons learned* and recommend improvements in how projects and portfolios are managed. Projects are prioritised against the company's stated strategic objectives (OKR- or balanced-scorecard-anchored).
- **Plan** — Using the customer's skills-and-resources database (internal + external roster), PM-AIgent proposes optimal resource allocation. Availability is adjusted according to project priority; assignments are semi-automatic — humans confirm.
- **Scenario planning** — On-demand what-if analyses (schedule slip, budget cut, resource loss, scope expansion) with quantitative impact on cost, time, quality and dependencies across the portfolio.
- **Documentation** — Automatic recording, transcription and structured summarisation of meetings, emails and chat threads; decisions and action items are extracted, assigned and tracked without manual capture.
- **Assess** — Conducts structured stakeholder interviews (async voice/text), performs targeted market and competitor research, and produces evidence-based project assessments to inform steering decisions.
- **Implement** — Designs and delivers training and adoption initiatives (onboarding paths, role-based enablement, change-communication packages) for the systems the project actually rolls out.

All features are available in English and German as standard. Additional languages on request at extra cost.

## 3 · Business Model

**Sales model.** PM-AIgent follows the enterprise-SaaS convention (per-seat licence + platform fee), differentiated from commodity PM tools by an agentic execution layer and by *value-based* pricing at the top of the range.

- **Team (up to 25 seats)** — EUR 49 / seat / month · platform fee included. Standard connectors (M365, Google Workspace, Slack, Jira, GitHub, Asana).
- **Business (25–500 seats)** — EUR 79 / seat / month · dedicated success manager, priority connectors, custom skills-database import.
- **Enterprise (500+ seats)** — custom pricing (typical EUR 60–120 / seat depending on deployment model), on-premises or private-cloud option, SSO/SCIM, dedicated model-governance environment.
- **Outcome-based add-on** — 3–5 % of documented savings on programme budget (measured against a baseline agreed at kickoff), capped at 2× the underlying SaaS fee. Open point: how savings are attributed to the agent rather than to other causes (e.g. before/after comparison with a control programme) is not yet defined.

|---|---|---|---|
| ClickUp (incl. ClickUp Brain, MCP-enabled) | ~USD 7 | custom | custom |
| Wrike | ~USD 9.80 | ~USD 24.80 | custom |
| Asana (Advanced) | ~USD 10.99 | ~USD 24.99 | custom |

PM-AIgent's price point (~2–3× the AI-scheduling category leaders) reflects that it replaces PMO-office capacity — a role the proposer puts at USD 90–140 k fully loaded per FTE (proposer's assumption, no source given). The customer's TCO comparison is agent-vs-PMO-headcount, not agent-vs-Asana. Note for the evaluation: freed PMO capacity only becomes a cash saving if positions are actually cut or measurably redeployed — until then it is capacity, not cost reduction.

**Existing developments — competitive baseline.** The AI-in-PM space has three distinct layers, all growing quickly:

| Layer | Representative players | Positioning |
|---|---|---|
| **PM-suites with embedded AI copilots** | Asana AI, Monday.com AI, ClickUp Brain, Wrike Work Intelligence, Smartsheet Aravo | AI as feature inside established PM suite; strong distribution advantage. Monday.com is Leader in Gartner's 2025 Magic Quadrant for both Adaptive PM and Collaborative Work Management. |
| **Agentic-PM native tools** | **Motion** (Automated Project Manager, re-prioritises roadmaps on schedule slip), Reclaim.ai, Timely | Built AI-first; narrower scope (mainly scheduling + task auto-prioritisation) but demonstrating agentic behaviour today. |
| **Foundation-model copilots at the office layer** | Microsoft Copilot for Project / Planner (Work IQ), Gemini for Google Workspace (formerly Duet AI), Notion AI | Ubiquitous distribution; bridge Outlook/Teams/Excel/Planner (or Docs/Sheets/Meet) but limited PM-specific depth. |

**Frontier trend (2026):** *agentic workflows* — agents that monitor Slack, GitHub, email and ticketing systems to update project state in real time, rather than waiting to be asked. Gartner projected in 2025 that **40 % of enterprise applications would feature embedded, purpose-built AI agents by the end of 2026** (up from < 5 % in 2025) — an analyst forecast that is itself due for checking now.

**Implication for PM-AIgent.** The window for a dedicated agentic-PM leader is open but closing fast. The layer above (Copilot, Gemini) will absorb generic PM tasks; the layer at the same level (Motion) is expanding scope. PM-AIgent's defensibility must come from (a) end-to-end programme scope, not just scheduling; (b) deep integration with the customer's skills-and-resources database and past-project archive; (c) outcome measurement that lets it argue ROI vs. PMO headcount, not vs. software licences.

## 4 · Business Parameters & Growth Potential

### Demand drivers („why now“)

Three forces converge to make 2026–2029 the defining window for agentic PM tools:

- **Adoption inflection.** The proposer cites PMI survey research: **more than 70 % of project organisations are already using or actively exploring AI**, but **only ~20 % of project managers rate their own AI skills as strong** — a demand pull created by organisations, not by the profession itself. Among high-adopter organisations, 91 % of respondents state improvements in quality, 87 % in scope, 86 % in cost and 85 % in schedule. These are shares of respondents (self-reported), not measured performance gains, and there is no comparison group — a correlation, not proof of cause. The figures could not be traced to the cited report; verify the exact PMI study and page before committee.
- **Agentic maturity.** Gartner projected in 2025 that 40 % of enterprise applications would have embedded, purpose-built AI agents by the end of 2026 (vs. < 5 % in 2025; analyst forecast). Motion has already shipped its „Automated Project Manager“ that re-prioritises entire roadmaps on a single schedule slip — this shows that vendors offer agentic PM today (vendor statement; effectiveness not independently verified).
- **Cost pressure on PMO headcount.** Enterprises are under active pressure to reduce PMO staffing; an agentic layer that keeps quality/coverage steady at 30–50 % of the current PMO cost (proposer's claim, to be proven in pilots) would be directly board-relevant.

| Player | Segment | Positioning | Threat level for PM-AIgent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Microsoft Copilot for Project / Planner (Work IQ)** | Foundation-model copilot | Ubiquitous in enterprise; bridges Outlook/Teams/Excel/Planner | Very high — distribution moat |
| **Gemini for Google Workspace** | Foundation-model copilot | Same layer for Google-native organisations | High |
| **Motion (Automated Project Manager)** | Agentic-PM native | Ships agentic re-prioritisation today at USD 29 | High — direct product competitor |
| **Monday.com AI, Asana AI, ClickUp Brain, Wrike WI** | PM-suite with AI | Leader class in Gartner Magic Quadrant 2025; embedded AI in market-leading distribution | Medium — different value promise (feature vs. replacement) |
| **Notion AI, Reclaim, Timely** | Adjacent AI-productivity | Narrower scope; unlikely to reach programme-level | Low |
| **Boutique agentic-PM startups** | Emerging | e.g. Epicflow, ClearFacts-style agents; small at present | Watch list |

**Frontier trend (2026):** *agentic workflows* replace status meetings and PMO check-ins. The winner is the player whose agent has (a) the deepest write-access to enterprise systems, (b) the strongest audit and governance envelope for regulated organisations, and (c) the cleanest cost story vs. traditional PMO.

**Differentiation PM-AIgent must prove.** (1) An agentic execution layer that owns the *end-to-end* project cycle — not just scheduling or documentation. (2) Deep integration with the customer's *own* skills-and-resources database and past-project archive (the moat is the customer's data, not the model). (3) Measurable PMO-cost displacement — a defensible metric agents can be judged on quarterly. (4) A demonstrable EU AI Act conformity path — for Annex III Nr. 4 this means internal control under Annex VI and an EU declaration of conformity; there is no certificate. An ISO/IEC 42001 certification can support the management system but does not replace the conformity assessment — see § 5.

**Governance open question.** „Optimize“ prioritises projects against strategy — that is portfolio work, not PMO support. Which decisions remain with the project sponsor and the portfolio board?

**Defensibility open questions.** Does PM-AIgent's differentiation hold once Copilot ships full agentic Project/Planner, or once Motion scales its „Automated PM“ upward into programme scope? Where does the moat sit — model, data, workflow depth, or governance evidence?

## 5 · Open items & risks — to develop step by step

**Product & pilots**

- **Reference customers.** At least 3 controlled pilots (recommended: 1 DACH mid-market enterprise, 1 professional-services firm, 1 industrial programme) with independent measurement of PMO-time saved, project throughput and quality of outcomes.
- **Human-in-the-loop policy.** Which decisions is the agent allowed to take autonomously (task assignment, calendar reshuffle, budget line reallocation < X %)? Which require named human approval (staffing changes, contract-scope decisions, risk-level escalations)? This design decision is regulatory-critical (see EU AI Act below).
- **Failure modes.** What happens when the agent misreads intent, loses connectivity to a source system, or receives contradictory instructions from stakeholders? Rollback semantics for agentic write-actions are a non-trivial engineering problem.
- **Skills-DB bootstrapping.** Cold-start problem: how does the agent become useful for a customer whose past-project archive is thin or non-digital?

**Regulatory & compliance**

- **EU AI Act.** Any PM-AIgent function that allocates work to specific individuals based on behaviour or personal characteristics falls under **Annex III Nr. 4 (employment / worker management) — high-risk AI**. Compliance obligations: risk-management system, data-governance, transparency, human oversight, logging, post-market monitoring. Obligations for Annex III high-risk systems apply from **2 December 2027** (Art. 113 AI Act as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744). BaFin's press release of 29 July 2026 confirms that date. Transparency obligations already apply from 2 August 2026. AI-literacy obligations (Art. 4) apply from 2 February 2025. Important for the design decision: a „recommend-only“ mode does **not** change the classification. Allocating work to individuals on the basis of skills or behaviour remains high-risk under Annex III Nr. 4 (b) even as a mere recommendation, and because skills profiles are profiling, the exception in Art. 6 (3) does not apply. The „Documentation“ feature (evaluating meetings, emails and chats) can also fall under Nr. 4 (b) as monitoring of performance and behaviour. „Limited risk“ is not a category of the AI Act. The classification only changes if the product no longer allocates work to, or evaluates, identifiable persons.
- **GDPR.** Meeting recordings, email/chat parsing and personnel-skill profiles are all personal data. Art. 6 legal basis, Art. 9 for special-category data, Art. 22 for solely automated individual decisions with legal or similarly significant effect — recommendations fall under it when they decisively shape the human decision (CJEU C-634/21, SCHUFA) —, Art. 35 DPIA mandatory in most deployments. Meetings may only be recorded with transparent information and, where required, the consent of the participants; in Germany, recording the non-public spoken word without authorisation is a criminal offence (§ 201 StGB). Design decision: on-device summarisation vs. cloud inference, and where the training-data pipeline lives.
- **Works-council mitbestimmung.** In DACH markets, § 87 (1) Nr. 6 BetrVG (technische Überwachung) triggers co-determination as soon as a tool is objectively suited to monitoring performance or behaviour — an intention to monitor is not required. Sales cycle and rollout must plan for co-determination negotiations — often a 3–9-month delay if not scoped early.
- **Confidentiality & IP.** Project data is typically the most sensitive commercial data a company holds (M&A, R&D, restructuring). Enterprise contract must ring-fence customer data from model retraining and cross-tenant leakage.

**Go-to-market**

- **Land-and-expand model.** Recommend starting at PMO-office level (10–30 seats) with a 90-day paid pilot; expand to programme-wide once ROI documented. Enterprise sales cycle typically 6–9 months.
- **Distribution options.** Direct sales (own sales force, high margin) vs. partner-led via consulting firms (Accenture, Capgemini, Sopra Steria — pre-existing PM practices) vs. marketplace listing (Microsoft AppSource, Salesforce AppExchange). Recommended: hybrid — direct for enterprise anchor customers, partner-led for mid-market volume.
- **Buyer personas.** CIO/CTO for platform decision; PMO director for functional decision; CFO for the outcome-based conversation. All three must be addressed.

**Unit economics & business plan**

- Customer acquisition cost (CAC): expected ~EUR 15–40 k / customer for direct enterprise sales; ~EUR 2–5 k for mid-market via marketplaces.
- Lifetime value (LTV): the proposer calculates EUR 79 / seat × 30 seats × 36 months × 90 % ≈ EUR 76 k per customer (Business tier). That is revenue, not contribution margin. Calculated properly: annual revenue per customer EUR 28,440 × gross margin × retained share per year. With an assumed gross margin of 70 % and 10 % annual churn over three years: 28,440 × 0.70 × (1 + 0.9 + 0.81) ≈ EUR 54 k. Against a CAC of EUR 15–40 k this gives an LTV/CAC ratio of roughly 1.3–3.6. Gross margin and churn are assumptions to be replaced by measured values.
- **Return on Token / Return on Inference:** cost per user-day for cloud inference is a critical KPI — the proposer assumes USD 0.10–0.50 per active-user-day for agentic workloads, falling ~40 % annually through 2028 (assumption without source, date or model). Cost per user-day = tokens per task × price per token; agents consume a multiple of the tokens of a simple chat, so a falling token price does not automatically lower the cost per user-day. Measure both in the pilot and calculate one case without any price decline.
- **Cost block still missing:** inference, monitoring, human oversight, support and other cost of goods sold are not yet shown per category (TCO).
- Financial projections 3–5 years with the three revenue-mix scenarios above.

**Fit with AIktivInvest thesis**

- Ticket size and burn rate: capital-efficient compared to hardware plays (IC 02) — mostly headcount + cloud, not manufacturing. Series-A range typical for the SaaS-agentic segment.
- Exit thesis: strategic acquirer (Microsoft, Atlassian, Monday.com, ServiceNow, workday) vs. late-stage growth financing.
- Timeline to break-even: **year 3–4** at mid-market cadence; **year 4–5** if enterprise-heavy (longer sales cycles, higher deal sizes) — proposer's estimate; no cost plan or calculation has been submitted yet.

## 6 · Organizational Structure

**Rationale.** PM-AIgent is a SaaS + agentic-AI product with enterprise-grade compliance obligations and a consulting-adjacent implementation motion. The organisation is built around **five accountable pods** with a lean corporate centre. Target headcount at Series-A: ~33 FTE, growing to ~78 by end of year 3.

### Leadership (5 FTE)

| Role | Accountability |
|---|---|
| **CEO** | Strategy, board, anchor-customer relationships, fundraising |
| **CTO** | Overall technology architecture (agentic orchestration + LLM stack + connector fabric + safety) |
| **COO / Head of Regulatory** | EU AI Act + GDPR + BetrVG compliance path; enterprise security posture; delivery ops |
| **CRO / Head of Commercial** | Direct enterprise sales, partner ecosystem, marketing, customer success |
| **CFO** | Financial planning, investor relations, unit economics, contract commercials |

### Product & engineering pods

| Pod | Headcount Y1 → Y3 | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| **Agent orchestration & LLM** | 7 → 16 | Multi-agent orchestration, planner/executor loop, foundation-model integration and fine-tuning, safety envelope |
| **Connectors & platform** | 5 → 12 | Enterprise integrations (M365, Google Workspace, Slack, Jira, GitHub, Asana, ServiceNow, SAP, Workday), data pipelines, SSO/SCIM, on-prem/private-cloud options |
| **Domain & UX** | 4 → 10 | PM domain modelling, project-management best practice codification, dashboards, role-based UX for PMO / Sponsor / Team-Lead / Contributor |
| **Safety, Quality & Regulatory** | 4 → 10 | EU AI Act technical file, GDPR/DPIA templates, works-council collateral, ISO 27001 + SOC 2 + ISO 42001, penetration testing, post-market surveillance |
| **Commercial & delivery** | 8 → 25 | Direct enterprise sales, partner-manager team, solution architects, customer success, onboarding-implementation consultants |

### Advisory & governance

- **Customer Advisory Board** (5 external members): 2 PMO directors, 1 CIO, 1 works-council chair, 1 professional-services partner — meets quarterly.
- **KI-Beauftragte:r / AI Officer**: internal accountability for EU AI Act conformity, reports to COO with dotted line to CEO.
- **Data Protection Officer (external)** — GDPR compliance across all customer deployments.
- **Security Advisory** — recognised external CISO (fractional) until in-house CISO hires (year 2).

### Deployment & delivery

PM-AIgent is delivered as **multi-tenant SaaS by default**, with a **single-tenant private-cloud option** (on top of hyperscaler infrastructure) for enterprises with elevated data-residency or IP-sensitivity requirements. **True on-prem** is deferred until year 4, subject to > 5 pipeline requests.

## Glossary of financial terms

- **TAM / SAM / SOM** — total addressable market / the part of it the company can actually serve (region, segment) / the share it can realistically win.
- **ARPU (average revenue per user)** — here: average revenue per seat and year.
- **CAGR (compound annual growth rate)** — (end value ÷ start value)^(1 ÷ years) − 1.
- **CAC (customer acquisition cost)** — sales and marketing cost ÷ number of new customers won.
- **LTV (lifetime value)** — contribution margin a customer brings over the whole relationship: revenue per customer and year × gross margin × expected years as a customer (driven by the annual churn rate).
- **Churn / retention** — share of customers lost / kept per year.
- **TCO (total cost of ownership)** — all one-off, recurring and end-of-life costs of a solution.
